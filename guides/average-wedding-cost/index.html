Average Wedding Cost in 2026

Two published averages, kept apart. The Knot says $34,200. Zola says $36,000.

The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study puts average spending at $34,200 for couples married in 2025. The median is $24,000. The average per guest is $292, and the average guest count is 117. Zola's Wedding Cost Index puts the average at $36,000.

The Knot's national guest-count bands are a reference: 1–50 guests: $17,100. 51–100 guests: $27,200. More than 100 guests: $43,300. They are not the calculator's formula. Using the top band as a step would push a 117-guest wedding off the published $34,200 average.

Season averages, also national: January–March $33,200, April–June $34,300, July–September $35,400, October–December $33,200. Summer is the high season in that study. The season control on the budget calculator applies that national pattern on top of a local figure and says so.

The local number is the one to plan from. A state page has that state's published figures. The wedding cost calculator applies them to a ZIP and a guest count.

About this page and common questions

Common questions

What is the average wedding cost in 2026?

The Knot's average is $34,200, with a median of $24,000 and $292 per guest. Zola's average is $36,000.

Why is the average higher than the median?

The Knot median is $24,000 and the average is $34,200. Expensive weddings pull the average up. Half of couples in that study spent the median or less.

Does the season change the average?

In The Knot's national figures, July–September weddings averaged $35,400. January–March and October–December averaged $33,200.
