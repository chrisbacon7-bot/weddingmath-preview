A calmer way to price a wedding

Most wedding sites want a tour request before they show a number. This one starts with the number.

Every dollar figure is tied to a public source and a date, listed on the sources page.

Three levels, always labeled

City study. The Knot or Zola published a figure for that city. If both did, you see both. Chicago uses Zola's published guest-count curve, because that's the one study that prints the total at several guest counts.

State study. Everywhere else that still has a state figure. All 50 states have a Knot average. Zola publishes a range for 49 of them. North Dakota's Zola range was not on the page, so it is left blank.

Estimated. A metro with federal price levels but no wedding survey of its own. We scale the state figures by the metro's price level divided by the state's. The Knot version and the Zola version are scaled separately, then shown as a range. The label says estimated. It is not a survey of weddings in that metro.

Guest count

The Knot's city and state averages match a typical wedding, 117 guests nationally. Food is published per guest. Drinks, cake, rentals, favors, and invitations are treated as growing with the headcount. The venue, photographer, and similar costs stay put. At 117 guests, a Knot-based estimate matches the published average. The national guest-count bands ($17,100, $27,200, and $43,300) are a reference, not a step that would push a 117-guest wedding off the published average.
