How Much Does a Wedding Cost in New Jersey?

The Knot's 2026 average for a New Jersey wedding is $57,000. That is $22,800 above the national Knot average of $34,200.

The Knot

$57,000

Average spend in New Jersey. 2026 Real Weddings Study, article updated July 28, 2026.

Zola

$32,800–$49,200

Published budget range. Updated March 6, 2026. Shown beside The Knot.

Zola's published New Jersey budget range is $32,800–$49,200. Zola's national average is $36,000. The range covers both sides of Zola's national average.

The Knot's national average guest count is 117. The $487 figure is $57,000 divided by 117. It is that division, not a separate published New Jersey rate.

Using the New Jersey figures

At 117 guests, the Knot-based planning figure matches the published New Jersey average of $57,000. Other guest counts move food and a few other lines and leave the venue, photographer, and similar costs put. Zola's state range, when it exists, stays beside that figure.

New Jersey's all-items price level is 108.8, with the US at 100, from BEA Regional Price Parities for 2024.

Common questions

How much does a wedding cost in New Jersey?

The Knot's 2026 average for New Jersey is $57,000. That is $22,800 above the national Knot average of $34,200. Zola's published New Jersey budget range is $32,800–$49,200. It stays beside the Knot average of $57,000. The two are separate studies.

What is the wedding cost per guest in New Jersey?

The Knot does not publish a separate New Jersey per-guest rate. Dividing the state average of $57,000 by the national average guest count of 117 is $487. That division is labeled as math, not as its own published rate. The national published rate is $292.

Which cities in New Jersey have their own wedding cost figures?

No city in New Jersey has its own Knot or Zola wedding figure in these studies. Use the state average, or an estimated metro row if you are in one.

What if our New Jersey city has no wedding study?

The state average is the sourced starting point. A metro without its own study is estimated by multiplying the state figures by that metro's BEA price level divided by New Jersey's price level of 108.8 (US = 100). The page labels those rows estimated.

Every figure is cited on the sources page.
